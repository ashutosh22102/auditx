\begin{abstract}
% DRAFT abstract: all figures come from the review in Chapter 4. Update it if
% your checks of the included studies change any number or judgement.
Artificial intelligence (AI) has become a standard tool in e-commerce and platform-based business, yet its wide availability raises the question of how it can create an advantage that competitors cannot easily copy. Drawing on the resource-based view and dynamic capabilities theory, this thesis proposes that AI capability leads to sustainable competitive advantage through the dynamic capabilities of sensing, seizing and transforming, and evaluates to what extent published empirical evidence supports six hypotheses derived from this model.

A structured literature review was conducted. A search in OpenAlex identified 1,807 records, which were classified in two separate runs of a language model against explicit criteria (Cohen's kappa = 0.84 between runs). Of these, 120 studies were included, nine of them provisionally on the basis of their titles alone. Twenty of the 95 empirical studies could be appraised against nine quality criteria, and all results were classified with predefined decision rules.

The findings can be stated at three levels. First, the literature suggests that AI is associated with organisational outcomes, mostly through complementary capabilities such as agility, innovation capability and organisational learning; in some of the appraised studies the direct association between AI and competitive advantage became small or non-significant once these capabilities were included, whereas in others it remained. Second, the evidence partly supports the view that AI capability contributes to competitive advantage through such capabilities: no hypothesis is supported, and all six are at most partly supported, on very different bases. The direct relationships (H1, H2) rest on several studies of mostly unknown quality, each dimension-specific mediation (H3 to H5) on a single indirectly relevant study, and the higher-order mediation (H6) on studies of related capabilities rather than of dynamic capabilities themselves. Third, the proposed mediation through sensing, seizing and transforming and the persistence of any advantage over time remain unconfirmed: no study estimates a dimension-specific indirect effect on competitive advantage, most studies are cross-sectional, the quality of most could not be assessed, and only four empirical studies concern e-commerce or platform-based business, one of them on complementors that sell through platforms.

The thesis concludes that the existing evidence does not yet confirm the proposed mechanism for e-commerce and platform firms. Managers should therefore invest in the capabilities and governance surrounding AI rather than in the technology alone, and future research needs dimension-specific, longitudinal studies in this context.
